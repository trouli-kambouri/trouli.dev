Circuits, The Internet of Things, Embedded Systems

I had the absolute pleasure of meeting a very interesting individual on 22/10/2026 called Adrian McEwan, who is also the technical lead and co-creator of Museum in a Box, among many other fascinating projects, while attempting to make alterations to a hoodie at DoES Liverpool. I never did make those alterations, but I did get a brief introduction to programming microchips, a topic which I was completely clueless in, which started my interest in embedded systems. I mentioned an interest in making a simple desk LED, which I would turn red when I'm working and green when I'm not. (I could just order a colour-changing LED from anywhere, but then I wouldn't learn how to programme and make it myself). Adrian gave me some helpful insights and tips to get started. I went home and used some CAD Software called TinkerCAD to make some designs on a breadboard. Here's how far I have got: This circuit has two separate LEDs, one red and one green, with the idea that you would use two separate buttons to turn them on. This was a starting point, but ideally, I wanted one LED that would cycle through red and green; this avoids the confusion of having both the red and green light on simultaneously. Now we have the circuit and code for an RGB LED, with a button that cycles through red, green, and 'off'. And this is the same but with an LED strip, that was slightly more challenging than a single LED. Considering I have (or had?) no experience making circuits or programming in C, I am pretty happy with my progress so far. I need to learn more about delays and their significance, get my code checked, learn how to solder, and source the materials (probably from DOES). TBC!
